A documentary exploring the life and music of Count Basie, the American jazz pianist who built the golden age of swing jazz. Starting his career as a jazz pianist in his twenties, Count Basie met many top musicians in Kansas City, Missouri, joined the Blue Devils in 1928, and later honed his skills with the Bennie Moten Orchestra before forming his own Basie Orchestra. Fusing jazz and blues and revolutionizing rhythm, he was hailed as the 'King of Swing' and remains beloved by jazz fans and musicians across generations in Japan. The film focuses not only on his public triumphs but also on his private life, which Basie rarely discussed. Through home videos, photo albums, and letters from his family, it reveals his love for his daughter Diane, born with cerebral palsy, and his wife Catherine, who supported the African American civil rights movement. Featuring interviews with current Basie Orchestra members, friends like Quincy Jones and Annie Ross, and archival materials, the documentary traces his legacy and the turbulent social history of 20th-century America. Directed by British filmmaker Jeremy Marre, known for many music documentaries including 'Roots Rock Reggae.'
