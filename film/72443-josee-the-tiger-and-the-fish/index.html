ジョゼと虎と魚たち（2003）

Josee, the Tiger and the Fish

2003 · 監督: 犬童一心 · 117分 · ドラマ / ロマンス

田辺聖子の短編小説を犬童一心監督が実写映画化。妻夫木聡と池脇千鶴が共演し、足の不自由な少女と平凡な大学生の切ない恋の行方を描く。ごく普通の大学生・恒夫がアルバイトする麻雀店では、近所に出没する謎の老婆の噂が話題となっていた。その老婆は決まって明け方に現れ、乳母車を押しているのだという。明け方、恒夫は坂道を下ってくる乳母車に遭遇。近寄って中を覗くと、そこには包丁を振り回すひとりの少女がいた。ジョゼと名乗るその少女は足が不自由で、祖母に乳母車を押してもらい散歩していたのだ。不思議な魅力を持つジョゼに惹かれた恒夫は、彼女の家をたびたび訪れるようになる。

[Reason for choosing this film] I was a high school student who would wander endlessly around a TSUTAYA that could only be reached by car driven by my parents. I would carefully narrow down which five DVDs to rent for a thousand yen, as selectively as possible. "Josee, the Tiger and the Fish" was a film I picked up during that time. Josee, played by Ikeshima Chizuru, has a disability in her legs and lives in a wheelchair. Through her encounter with Tsuneo, a university student played by Tsumabuki Satoshi, Josee's world expands dramatically. But—at the time, I couldn't fully digest the emotions depicted in the film with just one viewing. Especially in the latter half, Josee's monologue on the shell-shaped bed—I watched it over and over throughout the entire one-week rental period. It was my first movie experience where I clearly recognized: "This depicts emotions I cannot possibly grasp as I am now." And regarding this film, there is perhaps an even more unforgettable viewing experience than the main feature itself. That is the commentary track by director Inudō Isshin and both Ikewaki and Tsumabuki. Now I live in a world where, far from "five DVDs for a thousand yen," I can watch tens of thousands of titles for a few thousand yen a month—but I find myself longing for the many bonus features that came with most rental products, which I lost in exchange for that convenience. I have an insatiable appetite for bonus features. Making-of footage and deleted scenes are fine, but above all, I adore commentary tracks where directors and cast members share behind-the-scenes stories while watching the film. And it was none other than "Josee, the Tiger and the Fish" that instilled this habit in me. The voices of the three in the commentary were overflowing with pride and excitement at having brought this film into the world. The behind-the-scenes stories revealed one after another were fascinating, but after watching, what I thought above all was: "I want to become an adult like them." At the time, I perceived becoming an adult and working as painful and difficult things. For someone like me, the sight of these three talking about their work with such light, effervescent joy seemed to shine dazzlingly, like pearls spilling from a shell.
