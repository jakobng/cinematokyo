戦場のボヘミアン　カメラマン　石川文洋

2026 · 監督: 河邑厚徳 · 88分

ベトナム戦争やカンボジア大虐殺を取材した報道カメラマン・石川文洋の半生を描くドキュメンタリー。 1938年に沖縄で生まれた石川文洋は、3歳の時に太平洋戦争が勃発し、一家で本土に移住する。1964年、文洋はわずか27ドルを持って日本を飛び出し、香港経由でベトナムに入国。27歳になる前日、銃撃された米軍少佐を撮ったネガをAP通信が買い上げ世界に配信し、“国際報道カメラマン・石川文洋”が誕生する。1969年に一時帰国すると、ベトナムでの米軍プレスカードが認められて沖縄米軍基地の取材を許され、後方基地としてベトナム戦争に参加する沖縄の姿を浮き彫りにした。1979年のポルポト政権崩壊後はカンボジアの首都プノンペンに入り、最新の実情をとらえた本「大虐殺」を発表。68歳の秋には心筋梗塞で倒れるも、病気を抱える人に希望をもってほしいとの思いから、3500キロにおよぶ日本縦断の旅を約10カ月で完全踏破。辺野古での撮影も10年以上にわたり続けている。ベトナム戦争終結50年を迎えた2025年、ベトナム政府から式典に招待されホーチミンを再訪した彼は、そこである人物に会う。 長時間のインタビューや多数の著書から引用したモノローグ、膨大な記録写真の数々を通し、何度も命を落としそうになりながらも最前線で撮影を続けてきた石川文洋の半生を振り返る。監督は「天のしずく　辰巳芳子“いのちのスープ”」などの河邑厚徳。俳優の滝藤賢一がモノローグを担当。

A documentary depicting the life of photojournalist Bunyo Ishikawa, who covered the Vietnam War and the Cambodian genocide. Born in Okinawa in 1938, Bunyo Ishikawa was three years old when the Pacific War broke out, and his family moved to the mainland. In 1964, with only 27 dollars to his name, Bunyo left Japan and entered Vietnam via Hong Kong. The day before he turned 27, the Associated Press purchased his negative of a shot-down U.S. military major and distributed it worldwide, and thus "international photojournalist Bunyo Ishikawa" was born. On a temporary return to Japan in 1969, his U.S. military press card from Vietnam was recognized, allowing him to report on U.S. military bases in Okinawa, highlighting Okinawa's role as a rear base participating in the Vietnam War. After the collapse of the Pol Pot regime in 1979, he entered the Cambodian capital of Phnom Penh and published his book "The Great Massacre," capturing the latest realities. In the autumn of his 68th year he suffered a myocardial infarction, but wishing to give hope to people living with illness, he completed a roughly 3,500-kilometer journey across Japan from north to south in about ten months. He has also continued photographing in Henoko for more than ten years. In 2025, marking 50 years since the end of the Vietnam War, he was invited by the Vietnamese government to a ceremony and revisited Ho Chi Minh City, where he meets a certain person. Through long interviews, monologues quoted from his many books, and a vast number of documentary photographs, the film looks back on the life of Bunyo Ishikawa, who continued shooting on the front lines even as he repeatedly came close to losing his life. The director is Atsunori Kawamura, known for films such as "A Drop of Heaven: Yoshiko Tatsumi's 'Soup of Life.'" Actor Kenichi Takito performs the monologues.
